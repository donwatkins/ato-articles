I spent a fascinating weekend at a conference where one question kept resurfacing: *How do we preserve our organization’s historical archives so future generations can actually access them?* One key discussion centered on capturing significant memories and experiences, and how to ensure those recollections are recorded and stored securely, while still maintaining privacy and enabling future access.

This conversation naturally led us to explore the broader significance of open formats and open source solutions in our digital age. Given that most of us rely heavily on smartphones, we often store countless photos across various cloud services. Many of us use cloud-based document storage, and while we might be paying for these services, it raises an essential question: *How can we be sure that our images and other sensitive documents are protected from being scanned or utilized by third parties, particularly large language models that may exploit such data?* The implications of this issue for privacy and data security are profound and warrant serious consideration.

## From shoeboxes to the cloud: A brief history of memory-keeping

A couple of generations ago, most people stored their family or other memories in boxes filled with black-and-white and sometimes color photographs. Some even added names and dates on the back of those images, later sharing them with family or displaying them at graduations, weddings, and funerals. My dad recorded many of our family events using an 8mm movie camera, and a generation later, I captured our memories with a VHS camera.

Our public library received a grant to convert these films and photographs into digital formats, making them accessible on today’s devices. However, we must consider how to ensure that these memories won’t be locked away in a format that may become obsolete in five-to-ten years or less.

## Why open document formats matter

I frequently recommend open source solutions to help prevent vendor lock-in and ensure that your documents remain accessible to you. I am a user of <a href="https://www.libreoffice.org/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">LibreOffice</a> and suggest it to everyone I know because it is available on all operating systems.

I’ve helped people recover documents trapped in proprietary formats after the software that created them was discontinued. LibreOffice’s compatibility with legacy systems is remarkable and it opens dozens of obsolete formats dating back to the 1980s and 90s, including Microsoft Works (both MS-DOS and Windows versions), ClarisWorks, Apple Pages, WordPerfect, and Lotus WordPro.

## The case for open video formats

Open video formats play a crucial role in the accessibility and longevity of video content in our rapidly evolving digital landscape. Unlike proprietary formats that may tie users to specific software or platforms, open formats such as <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matroska" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Matroska</a>, <a href="https://www.webmproject.org/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">WebM</a>, and <a href="https://xiph.org/ogg/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Ogg</a> offer flexibility and ensure videos can be played across devices and applications without restrictions.

This openness not only fosters a collaborative environment where developers can innovate and improve video playback technologies but also safeguards against obsolescence. As technology changes, relying on open formats helps to ensure that valuable video memories and essential information remain accessible to future generations, free from the risks of vendor lock-in and proprietary barriers.

## Self-hosted alternatives to Google Drive and OneDrive

Open source alternatives to Google Drive and Microsoft OneDrive offer robust cloud storage options without the concerns associated with proprietary systems. Solutions like <a href="https://nextcloud.com/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Nextcloud</a> and <a href="https://owncloud.com/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">ownCloud</a> empower individuals and organizations to host their own file storage services, ensuring complete control over their data and enhancing privacy. Both are open source and can be self-hosted on your own hardware, either as virtual machines or as containers.

Nextcloud provides a self-hosted platform for file storage and synchronization, featuring robust collaboration capabilities across desktop, mobile, and web interfaces. Nextcloud Office is a robust online office suite built on LibreOffice, offering collaborative editing features. It supports all major formats for documents, spreadsheets, and presentations, and is compatible with all modern web browsers. Nextcloud Groupware brings together Calendar, Contacts, Mail, and various productivity tools to help teams work more efficiently, effortlessly, and on their own terms. With self-hosted cloud storage, your data is where you want it to be: at home or in the cloud you trust.

Self-hosting ownCloud delivers secure, private, and economical cloud storage tailored for small or home offices. It cuts out recurring per-user monthly fees, giving you absolute sovereignty over your data. ownCloud includes a comprehensive suite of built-in applications and more than 40 official marketplace extensions that expand its functionality far beyond simple file storage.

## Protecting memories for future generations

Preserving our historical archives and personal memories comes down to a commitment to open formats and open source solutions. Tools like LibreOffice, Nextcloud, and ownCloud protect our data from obsolescence while giving us more privacy and control over sensitive information. Embracing open formats isn’t just a technical choice; it’s a commitment to preserving our shared history and personal narratives in a way that honors their significance.
